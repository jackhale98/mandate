# Research: Run Length & Pacing Benchmarks

**Purpose:** Inform DEC-005 (time structure) and DEC-004 (run shape).
**Date:** 2026-11-18. Single research pass using web sources (listed at the end).
**Confidence:** Most figures are community or reviewer estimates, not telemetry. Where something comes from prior knowledge rather than this search, it is marked *(unverified)*.

## 1. Benchmarks

| Game | Closest unit to a "run" | Typical length | Structure notes | Relevance to Mandate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slay the Spire | Run | 30–45 min average by character; ~1.5 h with the true ending | 3 acts, boss at the end of each act | Acts with clear climaxes; short runs make failure cheap |
| Hades | Run | ~25–30 min for experienced players | Narrative advances across runs | Story that persists between failures |
| FTL | Run | 1–3 h; wins are uncommon | Sectors, with pressure to keep moving forward | Wins can be rare **only because** runs are short |
| Into the Breach | Run | 1–2.5 h (one player's report); HowLongToBeat main ≈ 5.5–6 h | Choose which islands to play and in what order | Flexible starting points and length |
| Against the Storm | Settlement, nested in a Cycle | Settlement 1–2 h (reports up to 3–5 h); a Cycle spans many settlements | Short, complete runs inside a longer arc with mounting pressure | **Closest structural analogue:** nested runs inside a meta-arc |
| Suzerain | Playthrough | 10–15 h; 9 main endings, 20+ sub-endings | Linear spine, branching outcomes | Players accept ~10 h political narratives if outcomes branch |
| Democracy 4 | Open-ended government | No fixed length; 3-month turns; ~4 h average playtime listed | Sandbox, ends on defeat or term limits | Quarterly turns work for national policy play |
| The Political Process | Career sandbox | ~39.5 h average total playtime (not per career) | 9 offices: city → state → national → president | Direct genre peer; a long, slow-building sandbox. Mandate should be the tighter, roguelike counterpart |
| Crusader Kings *(unverified)* | Dynasty campaign | 20–100+ h | Succession keeps the run going after a character dies | Failure redirects rather than ends the run |
| Reigns *(unverified)* | Reign | Minutes; reigns are chained into a dynasty | Very short units inside a longer chain | Extreme compression of political tenure |

**Session-length data (mobile):** Adjust reports average strategy-game sessions rising from ~31.7 to ~37.5 minutes (2025). GameAnalytics reports per-title medians of 5–6 minutes (top titles 8–9 minutes). The two use different methodologies. Takeaway: a natural stopping point every ~20–40 minutes, plus save-anywhere for very short sessions.

## 2. Findings

1. **Pure roguelike runs converge on 30–90 minutes, and strategic roguelikes on 1–3 hours.** A full career of 8–12 hours falls outside the roguelike norm and inside narrative-sim territory (Suzerain).
2. **Retry motivation depends on how much you lose when you fail, not on total length.** FTL can make wins rare because a loss costs about an hour. If a Mandate loss costs 8 hours, failure must not wipe the run, and every ending must be satisfying in its own right.
3. **Against the Storm's nested model fits the act structure.** Short, complete, self-contained units, each with its own climax, sit inside a longer arc that carries over. For Mandate, the roguelike unit should be the **chapter or act**, and the career is the arc.
4. **Players accept long political narratives if outcomes branch** (Suzerain). Procedural generation should give us branching structurally rather than through authored branches.
5. **Session design matters as much as run design.** Every ~20–40 minutes the player should reach a resolution point: an election result, the end of a legislative session, or a budget passed.
6. **The direct genre peer is a long sandbox.** A tighter, more roguelike structure is a point of differentiation for Mandate, not a deficit.
