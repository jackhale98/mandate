# Research: Unusual Systems of Government (Curveball Pool)

**Purpose:** Candidate archetypes and quirks for DEC-007, DEC-015, and DEC-017. Requested by the owner: "more unique or interesting" systems that have been used or proposed.
**Date:** 2026-11-18. One research pass; claims are verified where sources are listed. Items marked *(prior knowledge)* were not re-verified in this pass.
**Fictionalization rule:** every entry is adapted into the fictional world's institutions. No real parties, countries, or religious or ethnic groups appear in content.

Legend:
- **Kind:** A = archetype-level (reshapes the whole game) · Q = quirk (a modifier on an archetype).
- **Cost:** L / M / H = engine and content effort, assuming the DEC-017 primitives exist.

## 1. Executive curveballs

| Curveball | Real basis | What changes for the player | Kind | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Collegial executive (directorate)** | Switzerland's Federal Council; Uruguay's nine-member National Council of Government (1952–67), with 6 seats to the winner and 3 to the runner-up, and a rotating presidency | There is no single "top job". You win a seat on the council, then fight inside it. The Mandate Test becomes holding your bloc's seats | A | M |
| **Rotating presidency** | Uruguay's council presidency rotated yearly among majority members | The summit is a timed window: you get one year in the chair and must use it | Q | L |
| **Paired co-heads from opposing camps** | San Marino's two Captains Regent, elected every 6 months from opposing parties | You share the top office with a rival. Every major act needs a deal | Q | M |
| **Directly elected PM in a parliamentary system** | Israel 1996–2001, repealed because split-ticket voting fragmented the legislature and weakened coalition stability | You win personally but face a fractured legislature. A known failure mode is itself interesting gameplay | Q | M |
| **Premier elected by the legislature from independents (no parties)** | Nunavut consensus government: all candidates independent; members elect the Premier and up to 8 ministers by secret ballot | No party machine at all. Relationships with each legislator are everything | A | M |
| **Single non-renewable long term** | e.g. single six-year presidential terms *(prior knowledge)* | Removes re-election pressure but creates an instant lame duck. The Mandate Test moves to the party's succession | Q | L |
| **Ceremonial monarch or governor with reserve powers** | Many parliamentary systems *(prior knowledge)* | A rare, dramatic intervention: dissolution or refusing an appointment | Q | L |

## 2. Legislative and selection curveballs

| Curveball | Real basis | What changes for the player | Kind | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sortition chamber or citizens' assembly** | Permanent lottery-selected bodies (e.g. Paris, with 100 citizens); most sortition bodies are advisory, not binding | Members are random citizens drawn from the cohorts, with no campaigns and no parties. You persuade ordinary people, which is a direct use of the population model | Q (advisory) / A (binding) | M |
| **Functional or vocational seats** | Seats elected by occupational or sector constituencies *(prior knowledge)* | Economic sectors become electorates, so industry decline directly reshapes the legislature. A strong tie-in to the economy simulation | Q | M |
| **Quadratic voting for budget priorities** | Colorado House Democrats used it in 2019 to allocate ~$50M across 80+ bills; later challenged in court over open-meeting rules and replaced | Intensity matters: a small bloc that cares deeply can win funding. Used inside budget negotiation | Q | L |
| **Consensus or supermajority decision rule** | Consensus democracy: 51% is insufficient | Small factions gain vetoes. Coalition-building dominates | Q | L |
| **Consociational power-sharing** (fixed shares of offices for communal or regional blocs; mutual vetoes) | Power-sharing systems *(prior knowledge)*; fictionalize as regional or communal blocs | Concurrent majorities: a bill must pass in every bloc. Stability versus gridlock | A | H |
| **Top-two primary / fusion voting / approval voting / ranked-choice** | Various jurisdictions *(prior knowledge)* | Changes campaign strategy: appeal across the aisle, cross-endorsements, second preferences | Q | L–M (voting-rule plugins) |
| **Candidate vetting council** (an unelected body approves who may run) | Exists in some states *(prior knowledge)*; fictionalized | A hostile council can block your candidacy. Ties into Legal Jeopardy and Relationships | Q | M |

## 3. Direct-democracy and accountability curveballs

| Curveball | Real basis | What changes for the player | Kind | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Citizen initiatives and mandatory referendums** | Swiss-style direct democracy *(prior knowledge)* | Opponents can bypass you. You can bypass a hostile legislature. Campaigns happen between elections | Q | M |
| **Recall elections** | Several jurisdictions *(prior knowledge)* | Viability can collapse mid-term. A new terminal-ending path | Q | L |
| **Sunset clauses** (laws expire unless renewed) | Common legislative device *(prior knowledge)* | Your legacy must be defended, not just won | Q | L |
| **Compulsory voting** | e.g. Australia *(prior knowledge)* | Turnout mobilization matters less; persuasion of low-engagement voters matters more | Q | L |

## 4. Proposed-only (speculative) curveballs

| Curveball | Proposal | What changes for the player | Kind | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Futarchy** ("vote on values, bet on beliefs") | Robin Hanson: policies adopted iff decision markets predict they improve a voted welfare measure; never implemented | Markets, not legislators, decide policy adoption. You campaign on *values* and lobby *forecasters*. Uniquely suited to a simulation whose outcomes are computable | A | H |
| **Liquid democracy** (delegable votes) | Used inside some parties *(prior knowledge)* | Power brokers accumulate delegated votes. Relationships with "super-delegates" matter | Q (party-internal) | M |
| **Lottocracy** (all legislators chosen by lot) | Academic proposals *(prior knowledge)* | Extreme version of the sortition chamber | A | M |

## 5. Recommendations

- **First curveball archetypes after the MVP:**
  - **Collegial Directorate.** Highest novelty for modest cost, and it reframes the summit.
  - **Consensus / no-party legislature.** Pure relationship play, which showcases DEC-010.
- **First quirks:**
  - Recall
  - Citizen initiatives
  - Sortition advisory assembly
  - Functional seats
  - Sunset clauses
  - Rotating presidency
  - Paired co-heads
  - Ranked-choice / approval voting rules

  Each is mostly configuration once the DEC-017 primitives exist.
- **Defer:** consociational power-sharing (high cost and sensitivity; needs careful fictionalization) and futarchy (needs market actors), unless the owner prioritizes them.
