# Legal groundwork

**Not legal advice.** The author of this document is an AI coding assistant, not a lawyer. It collects facts about the project and the rules that seem to apply, so that a lawyer can review them quickly. Every rule cited here changes; each is flagged "verify the current rule". Nothing here has been reviewed by a lawyer. Searches were done on 2026-11-23.

Status of the work (M6 note 14): privacy page written and linked; third-party notices generated; fiction disclaimer added; AI-assistance statement drafted but **not live** (the owner decides the wording); name check done at a light level.

## 1. Privacy

Facts (details in `docs/development/SECURITY_REVIEW.md` section 6):
- No account, analytics, advertising, cookies or remote requests. The CSP forbids any remote origin.
- Stored on the device: saves and a random device id (IndexedDB `mandate`), cached street layouts (`mandate-town-plans`), four display preferences (localStorage), the game's own files (Cache Storage).
- The host, GitHub Pages, may log IP address and browser details. Not controlled by Mandate. [GitHub General Privacy Statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).
- A bug report is a local file (seed, every move, the save, recent errors, screen size, browser name and version, the player's text). The player chooses whether and where to send it. When one arrives by email or an issue, the developers become holders of personal data (an email address, possibly a name in the text); the privacy page tells players not to include anything they would not want read.
- Deleting: per run on the title screen; everything by clearing site data. There is no "delete all saves" button. Recommended for the settings (small task).

Written: `web/landing/privacy.html` (built to `<site>/privacy.html`), linked from the landing footer, the title screen and the in-game pocket menu. Fluent keys `ui-privacy`, `ui-privacy-note` (`content/base/locale/en/legal.ftl`).

When optional account sync arrives (DEC-104, deferred), the privacy page must be rewritten **before** it ships. It will then involve: an account identifier (passkey credential id, perhaps an email for recovery), save files stored on Cloudflare R2 and metadata in D1 (Cloudflare becomes a processor; list it, with the region), IP addresses in Cloudflare logs, a retention period, a lawful basis (contract/consent) and a data-processing agreement with Cloudflare, "delete my account and data" (already in the DEC) with a stated time limit, an export route (saves are already exportable), data-subject-rights contact, and the recovery code. The CSP's `connect-src` must name the sync origin, and the "nothing is sent to a server" sentences change. Players under the digital age of consent are a question for the lawyer. Syncing is opt-in; the game must keep working without it. Verify the current rule: GDPR/UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, COPPA if children are a target audience.

## 2. AI-assistance disclosure

Facts to base the wording on (owner to confirm each):
- Code, text and tests were written by AI coding assistants (Claude) under the owner's direction and review. The game design and decisions (`docs/design/decisions`) are directed by the owner.
- Art: DEC-084 D4 "no AI-generated pixels in shipped art; mood boards only". The pixel art is made by `tools/pixelkit`: parts are drawn as text grids in code and composed procedurally (recoloured, outlined, lit) by deterministic code. The grids were written by the AI assistants, so "drawn by hand" in the tool's comments means by a person or an assistant writing the grid, not by an image model. The owner should confirm that no image-generation model output is in the shipped pack, and say whether the grids were written by the assistant (the statement below says so).
- Players do not interact with an AI at play time: all text is pre-written (Fluent files), all outcomes come from the deterministic simulation. No model runs in the game.

### Draft statement (for the owner to approve; not live)

Short form (landing footer, store page):

> Mandate was made with AI assistance, under human direction. The code, the writing and the pixel-art parts were produced with the help of AI coding assistants; the design decisions, the editing and the testing were done by a person. The game's pixel art is composed by the game's own procedural tool, not by an image generator. Nothing in the game is generated by AI while you play: every line of text is written in advance and every outcome comes from the game's own rules.

Longer form (about page or store description, if wanted): the same, then "People, parties, towns and events are fictional and generated by the game."

Owner decisions: whether to use "made with AI assistance" or "AI-generated"; whether to name the tool; whether the art sentence is accurate; where it appears (footer, about, store).

### Platform and legal rules (all: verify the current rule)

- **Steam.** Developers fill in an AI-use disclosure in Steamworks, split into pre-generated content (made with AI tools during development: art, code, sound) and live-generated content (made while the game runs, which also needs a description of guardrails and a player reporting route). Valve clarified in January 2026 that the form targets AI-generated content players consume, not behind-the-scenes efficiency tools. Written text shown on the store page. For Mandate: pre-generated text and possibly code; no live-generated. Likely answer: disclose pre-generated, with the statement above. Sources: [PC Gamer on the January 2026 form change](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/), [GamingOnLinux, 2024 rules](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/01/valve-announces-new-rules-for-games-with-ai-content-on-steam/). Primary source: Steamworks documentation, "Content Survey" / AI disclosure.
- **itch.io.** A "Generative AI disclosure" field on the project page; "yes" adds an AI Generated tag (sub-tags for graphics, sound, text and dialog, code), "no" adds a No AI tag. Undisclosed AI content may be dropped from browse pages. Coverage disagrees on whether it applies to games or only assets. For Mandate: "yes" for text and code, "no" for graphics and sound if the owner confirms the art statement. Sources: [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/software/platforms/indie-distribution-platform-itch-io-now-requires-asset-creators-to-disclose-the-use-of-generative-ai-in-their-work), [itch.io forum thread](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging?before=39). Primary: itch.io quality guidelines, AI disclosure section.
- **EU AI Act, transparency (Article 50).** Reported as applying from 2 August 2026, with a transition to 2 December 2026 for marking duties on older generative systems, and Commission guidelines of 20 July 2026. The duties fall on providers of systems that generate synthetic content, on deployers of deep fakes and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, and on systems that interact directly with people. A game whose AI-assisted content is fixed at development time, with no AI interacting with the player, seems unlikely to be a "provider" or "deployer" in that sense, but fictional political text that looks real is exactly what a lawyer should look at (the fiction disclaimer helps). If a live language model is ever added, Article 50 applies directly (tell players they are talking to an AI; mark generated text). Sources: [Cooley](https://cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-03-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-take-effect-2-august-2026), [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august). These are law-firm summaries, not the Official Journal text (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).
- **Copyright.** AI-generated output may not be copyrightable in some jurisdictions (the US Copyright Office requires human authorship). Code and text written by an assistant under heavy human direction and editing is a grey area; art that is composed by the tool from parts is likewise. Matters for the licence choice below and for any claim of exclusive rights. A lawyer question.

## 3. Licences and notices

- `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` (repo root) lists 46 Rust crates compiled into the engine and 11 npm packages bundled into the app. Result: everything is permissive (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD-2, 0BSD, Zlib, Unlicense, CC0, MIT-0, Unicode-3.0). No copyleft, no unusual terms in what ships. Linked from the landing footer. Apache-2.0 components (Dexie, Fluent) ask that the licence text travel with copies; the file links the sources. Before a store release, attach full licence texts (generate with `cargo about` or `cargo deny` and an npm licence tool).
- Fonts: none shipped (system fonts). Art: own pack; no third-party art, icons or sound.
- **The project has no licence file at its root.** Not choosing. Options and what they mean:
  1. All rights reserved (the default with no file; state it in a `LICENSE` file). Public source can be read but not reused. Matches `art/mandate` being "all rights reserved" (DEC-084 D8). Simplest for a commercial game.
  2. Source-available with limits (for example PolyForm Noncommercial, or Business Source License): source readable and modifiable, commercial use restricted, time-limited conversion possible.
  3. Permissive open source (MIT or Apache-2.0) for code, with the art and content under a separate, more restrictive licence (this is what `tools/pixelkit`'s `package.json` already declares: "MIT OR Apache-2.0", while Mandate's pack is all rights reserved). The README should say which files each covers.
  4. Copyleft for code (GPL-3.0, AGPL-3.0): forks must stay open; hard to put on some stores (GPL and app stores); AGPL covers network use.
  Also decide: whether to accept outside contributions (a contributor agreement or DCO), and the copyright holder's name for the notice. Note `pixelkit` already says MIT OR Apache-2.0 with no licence file beside it.

## 5. The name "Mandate"

Light check only, by web search. **A real clearance search (USPTO, EUIPO, UKIPO, WIPO, plus common-law use and app stores) is a lawyer's job.**

- Game titles already using the word: [Deck of Mandate](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3907900) (Steam, political strategy in a fictional medieval republic, released 2025); [The Mandate](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4865580) (Steam, unreleased, political strategy about the U.S. House); [Mandat](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4898150/Mandat/) (Steam, interactive fiction about governing a country in crisis); [Politiks](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3980980) (a political roguelite deckbuilder, different name, same niche). No game titled exactly "Mandate" and described as a political roguelike turned up in the first results.
- Trademark: a USPTO record for THE MANDATE, serial 86561595, computer and video game software, filed 2015, listed as abandoned in 2017 ([Justia](https://trademark.justia.com/865/61/the-86561595.html)). Abandoned filings do not block, but the mark may still have common-law rights, and a newer filing may exist that the search did not find.
- Assessment: "Mandate" is a common dictionary word and a political term, so it is a weak, descriptive-leaning mark that would be hard to own alone, and so also hard to stop others from using. The nearby political-game titles raise confusion risk at store level and make search discovery harder ("Mandate" on Steam returns the games above). Consider a distinguishing subtitle ("Mandate: A Political Career") for stores and keep the word "Mandate" for the game's own name. Do a professional search in the game and software classes (9 and 41) before spending on a trademark or a store page.

## 6. Other notices to consider

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Age rating | Self-assess before a store release. Themes: politics, corruption, bribery, crime (verify what the content actually depicts, including scandal and violence text). Likely PEGI 7-12 or ESRB E10+/T (mild themes, simulated gambling no); the IARC questionnaire (used by Google Play, Microsoft Store and others) produces ratings for several regions, and Steam asks its own questionnaire. Answer from the content, not this guess. |
| Accessibility statement | Optional and low-risk. Add a short page once the accessibility work is stable: keyboard play, screen-reader labels, reduced motion, colour contrast (the landing e2e tests check contrast), text size; known gaps; a contact for reports. Needed in some jurisdictions for commercial products (European Accessibility Act applies to some consumer services from June 2025: verify whether a game is in scope). |
| Cookie banner | **Not needed on current facts.** The game sets no cookies and uses local storage and a service worker only for the game's own function (saves, offline files, display choices), which privacy rules in the EU/UK generally treat as strictly necessary (verify the current rule: ePrivacy Article 5(3) and national guidance). No analytics or third-party content. A banner (and a consent flow) becomes necessary if analytics, ads, embeds or any non-essential storage is added. The privacy page already tells the player what is stored. |
| Contact address | Needed for reports, data requests and store pages. Currently the repository issue tracker. Before a commercial release, add a monitored email (a role address on the custom domain) and name the publisher or person responsible; stores and some laws require a legal contact. Owner decision. |
| Terms of use / EULA | Not needed for a free browser game; may be wanted for a store release. Include a no-warranty line and a save-loss note ("saves live in your browser; export to back up"). |
| Security contact | A `SECURITY.md` or `security.txt` with a reporting route. Easy; needs the contact decision above. |
| Imprint (Impressum) | Some countries (Germany, Austria) require a provider identification on commercial sites. A lawyer question if the owner or audience is there. |
