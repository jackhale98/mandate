## DEC-110 — Specific industries and named plants
- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-11-24: "I'm good with your recommendations"; design in `docs/design/INDUSTRIES.md`; owner's note 17, 2026-11-24: "People are more interested in something specific as opposed to 'manufacturing demand'.").
- **Context:** a plant is a town's share of one generic sector with no product, so a closure reads as "manufacturing layoffs". DEC-075 made sectors a registry and foresaw "industries with their own quirks".
- **Decision:**
  1. **Industries are a registry level under sectors** (`industries.jsonc`). The eight sectors stay as groups for totals, worries and map layers; a split sector's state is the sum of its industries. The base pack splits manufacturing, resources and agriculture into sixteen.
  2. **Demand per industry** is one generic product of named factors with exponents in content: the world cycle, the industry's own world index (a keyed stream: reversion, noise, rare jumps), commodity prices sold and bought, the resource stock, water, national competitiveness, local labour costs, and local supply links that read upstream jobs in the town and its region.
  3. **Named plants.** A town's larger employers are plants: a place of an existing kind with an industry, a product, a name in parts and capacity. Their people are characters at the place. Plant jobs, industry jobs and sector jobs reconcile exactly because totals are sums.
  4. **Closures are per plant, deterministic and explained:** short time, a notice with a date, a reprieve or the closure, each an event; the cause is the weakest factor of the industry's formula (the spine's `Pressure`). Plants also open: from idle labour and policy (an investment pool) and from booms.
  5. **Worldgen places industries by geography and regional tradition** with keyed draws; the burn-in is their history.
  6. **Policy can target an industry or one plant**, at any tier (keyed by `Jurisdiction`); plants themselves belong to a town.
  7. **No code names an industry, a product or a works.** Text takes the plant, product and factor as arguments; lines are written per status and factor, with four flavour lines for each industry.
- **Ready for foreign trade (owner, 2026-11-24: "We want trade to be well positioned for expansion to foreign trade and affairs in the future").** Built with the registry, at no change in behaviour while there is one market:
  1. **Markets as a registry.** An industry's external demand is a sum over markets of its share × the market's index for that industry × the market's access. Today there is one market, the rest of the world, with access 1. A neighbour (DEC-087, M7) becomes a market whose index follows its economy; a tariff, a treaty or a dispute (M8) changes access.
  2. **Exposure as content:** each industry's export share and its exposure to imports.
  3. **Goods as a registry,** so a tariff or a deal can name what it covers; the commodity price indices become the prices of the goods they stand for.
  4. **Gateways** (ports now; border crossings and rail heads later) as a property that siting can weight and a disruption can hit.
  5. **Causes name the market and the good,** so "the tariff closed the cannery" can be read off the spine.
  Not built now: bilateral flows with quantities, exchange rates, tariffs as policies, the foreign nations themselves.
- **Not this (later, after transport):** products as goods, firms and ownership, supply chains with quantities, trade between towns, prices per product.
- **Cost:** save v27 with a migration; state hashes and generated worlds change once; about 294 Fluent keys (145 sentences); an estimated 2–4% on world generation; the tasks in §11.
- **Changes:** DEC-075 stage 1 (sectors gain a child registry; `SectorState` becomes derived for split sectors); the closure rule (was a monthly chance below 0.85 on the sector's driver); `economy.manufacturing_trend` (per-industry trends); the spine's `Pressure` (four more).
- **Dependencies:** DEC-041, DEC-053, DEC-054, DEC-075, DEC-078, DEC-085, DEC-089, DEC-090, DEC-092, DEC-098, DEC-099; the causal spine (S1); the staged arcs design.
- **Answers to the design's open questions (owner, 2026-11-24, on the lead's recommendations):**
  1. Fishing sits under resources, not agriculture.
  2. Closures go by short time, a dated notice, then a reprieve or the closure, with no monthly roll. The review belongs to the economy; arcs (DEC-109) read it and push on it only through requests.
  3. A seed gives a different country afterwards. Accepted, before testers.
  4. Old saves get named plants only at the works they already have.
  5. No tourism or knowledge industries in this pass.
  6. Names and the status and cause lines are written first; flavour lines come with the crisis rewrite.
- **Not this (later, after transport):** products as goods, firms, supply chains and trade between towns.
- **Amended 2026-11-26 (owner): short time ends in a review.** Short time had no end for demand between the notice line (0.78) and the full-time line (0.94): in sixteen small worlds 258 plants were on short time at the start of play and none got a notice in 39 weeks; 143 had been on short time a year or more, the longest 19 years. Now, when a plant has been on short time `review_months` (12), the economy reviews it. If its margin-adjusted demand is still under `review_line` (0.88) and its industry can close, it gets a dated notice by the existing path (one keyed draw for the date; a reprieve possible; the town's notice gap holds, so a second plant waits on short time until the town is free, outside a recession). Otherwise it goes back to full time; if demand is still under the full-time line it sheds `review_cut` (0.10) of its capacity and that share of its jobs: the jobs that would not come back with orders. A fixed share, not one fitted to the shortfall, because jobs already follow demand month by month; what the review adds is the part that stays lost. An industry that never closes (farms, the fish quay) goes back to full time at its review whatever its demand, with the cut. The review is an event (`PlantReviewed`: months on short time, demand and the line in percent, the outcome, the jobs cut) on the plant's thread with the weakest factor as its cause; a notice from a review names it as its cause, so the Why reads "short time had run 12 months, and demand was still under 88% of normal". A reprieve starts a new spell of short time (its review is `review_months` later). The early paths stay: full time after two months over 0.94, and a notice after nine months under 0.78. The review stays the economy's: arcs read it and push on it only through requests. The three numbers are content (`economy.industries`) and the tuning pass's.
