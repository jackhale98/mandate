## DEC-115 — The town can break

- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-11-26). The owner asked whether a player can blow a town's budget by passing too many costly bills, and decided: "We should allow users to break the town in general as part of the game. They may just lose earlier, or have to save it from their own mistakes." On chance: "Shouldn't there be a little chance involved in roguelike spirit?"
- **Context:** Today a town cannot be broken. A new law is funded only from last year's room plus 3%; at the budget year programmes are capped at a quarter of the budget and pro-rated; baseline services shrink by at most 5% a year; the property tax rises to close the gap; debt grows only when cash goes negative or a family bill borrows. Budget stress (debt over a year's budget) stays 0 in bot play, so the fiscal-hole crisis (0.4) never fires. Overspending costs something, spread thin and late, and never becomes a moment.
- **Decision:**
  1. **A town can break, and the player can lose to it.** Finances first; the same rule applies to every domain that can run down (streets and faults, plants, hazards unprotected, and later people leaving, M7).
  2. **Every break is the player's to see:**
     - **Caused.** Every step is on the causal spine; "Why?" names the bills, borrowing and cuts that led to it.
     - **Warned.** It comes in stages, each shown: an underfunded law, rates up, a service cut, debt and its interest rising, lenders charging more, the fiscal hole.
     - **Recoverable until the end.** Every stage before the last can be saved at a price (cuts, a rate rise, selling an asset, a bailout with strings). The fiscal hole is a crisis arc the player can win.
     - **A real end.** A town that cannot pay is taken over by the tier above that the constitution names (an administrator from the region or the nation, through constitution ids, DEC-098). For a player who holds the executive post that is a loss: removed, and the career card says how. For a member it is a disaster rivals pin on them.
  3. **Luck can tip a town, never topple a healthy one.** Chance stays (shown odds, hazards, closures, rivals, booms). A shock can be the last straw for a town the player made fragile; the same shock to a town with reserves is a hard year. "Why?" reads it that way ("the flood broke a town with no reserve and no wall"). Luck also rescues: a boom, an employer, a grant.
  4. **The storyteller sets how hard luck hits:** Steady rarely lets luck tip anything; Classic lets it finish a fragile town; Unfiltered lets harsher luck hurt a careful town badly, never from full health in one blow.
  5. **It takes years, not a turn.** The warning stages are long enough that a single bad turn never breaks a town.
- **Consequences (first task, queued 2026-11-26):** the proposal sheet shows how much of a bill this year's budget can fund (`finance::first_share`, already computed for the impact estimate); "Why?" on a cut or a rate rise names the programmes that took the money; the fiscal hole made reachable and played as an arc; insolvency and the tier above's administration; a lab check that a spendthrift bot breaks its town within a term in most worlds and a careful one never does. The numbers are set in the M6 tuning pass.
- **Dependencies:** DEC-024, DEC-092, DEC-098, DEC-109, DEC-110, DEC-114.
