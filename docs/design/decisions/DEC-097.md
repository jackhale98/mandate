## DEC-097 — Engagement and variety

- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-11-19: "Yes let's do it"). The owner worried that day-to-day play would be repetitive ("canvas and pass an unemployment bill every day") and asked whether the storyteller's output had been audited. They also asked for "hidden Easter eggs or secrets in the actual town, so people who are walking are rewarded with something".
- **Evidence (lab audit, 2026-11-19):**
  - Two 4-year careers (`mandate-cli story`, seeds 777 and 2026, mixed bot) had 27 and 29 decisions from only 10 and 12 templates.
  - The same few repeated: amendment-for-a-vote ×5, jobs plea ×5, pollution ×3–4, traders' meeting ×3–4.
  - Each career had one storyline (the rival), no press stories about the player, no scandals, and quiet stretches of up to 168 days.
  - The bot tournament (K4): canvass-only wins 84–87% of careers, the most of any strategy.
- **Decision:**
  1. **Ground game rebalance.** Canvassing mainly raises awareness, with fast diminishing returns per neighbourhood and per turn. Approval comes mostly from delivery: credit and blame for laws (DEC-094 V1), kept promises, and help to individuals. Canvassing stays strongest in campaign season.
  2. **Asks and promises (a goals layer).** People the player meets, the party, the broker, organisations and the press make concrete asks with deadlines ("fix the drain on Mill Lane", "vote with us on the budget", "say something about the closure"). Rewards are relationships, record, endorsements and knowledge; a broken promise costs. Campaign promises become tracked commitments. They are generated from live state and characters' goals (DEC-032) as data-driven ask templates, and shown in the pocket's Today tab.
  3. **Storyteller variety:**
     - a per-template cooldown, and no repeat within a chapter;
     - a larger pool, including consequences of the player's own past choices;
     - 2–4 storylines per career, with NPC schemes (rival, broker) running in parallel;
     - the press covers the player's work (bills passed, promises kept or broken, appearances), not only scandals.
  4. **Secrets (information).** Town secrets are uncovered by asking around, investigating, or tipping off the press, building on knowledge (DEC-091) and conversations (DEC-093): broker deals, buried reports, who backs whom, a councillor's debts. They are seeded from live state, never decisive alone, and follow "the world does not wait" (secrets exist and matter whether found or not).
  5. **Walking rewards (exploration).** Things found only by walking the town (owner request):
     - hidden places (DEC-090 D7);
     - curiosities and Easter eggs: a plaque, a mural, an old campaign poster, a lost cat, a bench with a story, a view from the hill;
     - rare ambient encounters: a resident with a tip, a journalist off duty, the broker taking a walk;
     - small collectible town lore for the notebook.

     Rewards are small: knowledge, a lead, goodwill, a story beat, an occasional modest boost. They are never required, and their kinds are data-driven so they vary by town. Quick mode and the Places tab reach anything that affects play. Pure flavour may stay walk-only, but nothing that affects play does (DEC-051).
  6. **An engagement gate in the lab.** Per simulated career:
     - distinct decision templates and repeats per template;
     - the longest quiet stretch;
     - storylines;
     - press stories about the player;
     - asks offered, kept and broken;
     - the action mix of winning bots: no single-verb strategy may be the best overall, which replaces today's canvass dominance.

     Thresholds are set from the design pass, and it runs in CI beside the bot gates.

- **Owner answers (2026-11-19: "Go with recommendations"):**
  1. Canvassing between elections still raises approval a little: about 0.6× today on a first visit, fading fast on repeat visits.
  2. Casework comes only through asks, not as a free-standing action.
  3. Three campaign promises. Broken public promises make the press and give the rival material.
  4. No using a secret to pressure someone in M6. It may come later as a shady act with its own hidden fact.
  5. Easter eggs are in-world only, with no completion reward for collecting lore.
  6. The engagement gate is report-only (nightly) until E2–E8 merge, then it blocks CI.
- **Balance principle (owner, 2026-11-19).** The owner said: "isn't the explorer bot how we intend people to actually play the game? If it was balanced perfectly so a strategy of just canvassing worked just as well as going around and doing different things wouldn't that be bad game play?"

  Agreed. Players optimise toward whatever wins, so the winning way to play must be the engaging one. Balance does not aim for equal strategies. It aims for:
  1. **A clear skill gap.** Informed, varied play (reading the town, mixing actions, following up) beats every rote routine (a single repeated verb, or a blind mix) by a margin a player can feel.
  2. **Variety inside good play.** Several informed styles exist (for example a coalition builder, a legislator, a campaigner), and which one is best depends on the town and its situation. No one of them wins everywhere.
  3. **The gates police rote play:** canvassing only, media only, asks only, and corruption.
  4. **Walking is a way in, not a requirement.** Gathering knowledge pays a lot. Gathering it by walking rather than through the Places tab pays only a little (DEC-051 parity).

  This amends the reading of DEC-077 §5's variety gate. Variety is measured among the informed styles, not between the intended way of playing and the rote routines. The gate ladder (ENGAGEMENT §7.2) is enforced in steps as E1, E8, E9 and E10 land, and E12 tunes the thresholds.
- **Research folded in (owner, 2026-11-19: "Yes do it").** The proposals of `docs/design/research/ENGAGEMENT_REFERENCES.md` §3 are accepted into ENGAGEMENT.md and tasks E1–E12:
  - **A pacing cycle for the director,** after Left 4 Dead's AI Director and RimWorld: build-up, peak and relax within each chapter. Storyteller profiles change how often things happen, not how severe they are.
  - **Chapter themes and priority tiers for templates:** closing storyline and consequence windows come first, then the chapter's theme. No theme repeats in consecutive chapters.
  - **Conversation lines that remember,** after Hades: priority, once-only lines and callbacks to what the player did, with a bounded `heard_once` state.
  - **One ladder of balance gates** encoding the balance principle:
    - rote < blind mix < informed play;
    - knowledge matters (omniscient vs blind);
    - several informed styles each win somewhere.
  - **Knowledge as the progression,** after Outer Wilds:
    - every find leads to another;
    - lore ties to what the player already knows;
    - a rival's next move is shown once known (Into the Breach);
    - per-template telemetry.
  - **Smaller additions:**
    - asks offered at a sliding rate;
    - a rising bar in the second term;
    - pressures introduced year by year;
    - at least 6 campaign-only templates.

- **Note (E2, lead, 2026-11-20): the climax fallback, until E3 widens the pool.** E2 made the climax respect cooldowns and climax memory (ENGAGEMENT §4.1). With today's 31 templates a session in the middle of a career can find no grounded situation left to climax on (every miss in the lab had its climax peak and its forced retry run with no candidate): mixed careers had a climax in 83% of chapters, the pacing test's honest player 74%, against DEC-005's "about one climax per chapter". So:
  - when a climax peak (or its forced retry) has no eligible candidate, it is drawn again with template cooldowns waived. Climax memory, the last chapter's climax (always, whatever `climax_memory_chapters` says), no repeat within the chapter, pending decisions and `min_career_days` still hold, so no climax repeats in consecutive chapters;
  - a borrowed climax takes a template's second or third use, never a fourth, draws among the templates whose return it delays least, and cools the template for its base cooldown (`max(floor, cooldown)`), not a grown one;
  - the phase log records it (`DirectorPhase.climax_fallback`); `story --metrics` counts it (`climax_fallbacks`, median 1 per 5-year career).

  The cost: a template may reach four uses in five years in a thin town (2 of 12 lab careers; the §7.2 gate says ≤ 3). E3 retires the fallback's need; the lab will show it falling to 0.
