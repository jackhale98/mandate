## DEC-102 — Difficulty and run structure
- **Status:** Accepted (owner, 2026-11-23, with the implementation plan). The owner asked to balance difficulty "between its length, its roguelike roots, and how engaging and replayable it is". Playtests may move the numbers.
- **Context:**
  - A full career runs 6–10 h (REQ-008), longer than most roguelike runs.
  - Today a lost seat ends Act 1.
  - The bot gate ladder, the idle cap and storyteller profiles exist, but there are no agreed win-rate targets.
- **Proposal:**
  1. **A run is a career.**
     - Losing a seat ends the act with an epilogue, and the career continues into a comeback, which is itself a DEC-101 opening.
     - The run ends on a final defeat: jail, total disgrace, or two losses in a row.
  2. **Targets, measured by bots and then playtests:**
     - an idle player wins at most 10%;
     - an engaged newcomer is re-elected about 60–70%;
     - a skilled player is re-elected about 85%;
     - reaching the top office is rare (about 5–15% for skilled players);
     - Act 1 lasts 1.5–2.5 h with skips.

     Each storyteller and difficulty preset gets its own gates in CI.
  3. **Difficulty levers as data:** rival competence, what the player knows at the start, event harshness, economic volatility, how long voters remember, and scandal rates.
  4. **Modes:** Story (forgiving; skips encouraged), Standard, and Ironman (one save, no rewinds).
  5. **Replay** comes from variety (DEC-100, DEC-101), mastery, comebacks, the Legacy record and returning to a world (DEC-103). Legacy unlocks stay deferred.
- **Owner's additions (2026-11-24):**
  6. **Difficulty is chosen by the start, and rated by measurement.** The main difficulty control is where and as whom the player begins (their party's standing in the town, the town's state, the holder's strength), offered on the opening draft (DEC-101). A start's rating is a win rate measured by the stand-in players (STAGED_ARCS A4), shown with the two or three facts that drive it; no start is labelled hard or easy by hand, because a town in crisis under an unpopular holder can be the easiest place to run. A start nobody can win (a minority party in a town that never changes party) is not offered until the one-party fix makes it winnable. The modes of clause 4 change only forgiveness (rewinds, how fast voters forget), never the opposition. The career is one continuous play with one character, from the town to the national body and the top office. When that same character considers a race for a higher post, the race is rated the same way (their record, standing and party as they are, against that seat), so the choice of which race to enter and when is the difficulty choice at every tier. Nothing resets.
  7. **Skipping holds ground; it never wins it.** A career played only by skipping lands between idle and rote play. The gate `proxy_not_optimal` (today: no better than the median informed style, which allowed 62.5% first-election wins when skips were built) is replaced by two bars: a skip-only first-election win rate no higher than the best single-action routine's, and a skip-only re-election rate clearly below the engaged newcomer's (about 40–45% against 60–70%). A skip never makes one of the player's choices: the stand-in does upkeep only (votes by the directives, holds standing), learns nothing new, makes no promises and files for no higher post; an ask waits and stops the skip when it comes due, and every stage of a staged arc stops it. So that skips stay worth using, a third bar: a stand-in that skips the quiet weeks and plays each stop well lands within a few points of one that plays every turn. And a floor on how long a typical skip runs in a quiet stretch (`act-length`), so stops do not come every week. Built with the stand-ins and grader (A4); the numbers are measured first and may move.
- **Dependencies:** DEC-005, DEC-006, DEC-076, DEC-097, DEC-100, DEC-101, DEC-103.
